    At the table

      Roulette rules

      One wheel for everyone: every 45 seconds there are 30 seconds to place chips, then the ball is thrown. While it rolls (4 more seconds) you cannot add chips, but you can drag your stacks to another field or swap two of them. Then: no more bets, and everybody sees the same number. The chips of the other players show on the felt as small coloured markers. Pick a chip, tap the table to put it down, then press Place bets (chips still waiting when the time runs out go in by themselves). You can place more until the time is up. European wheel with one zero. A single number pays 35 to 1. Red or black, odd or even, 1–18 or 19–36 pay 1 to 1. A dozen (1st, 2nd, 3rd 12) or a column (2:1 under the numbers) pays 2 to 1. Zero loses every outside bet. Winnings land on your balance as soon as the ball stops, even if you left the table.

      Pick a bet and drop a ball.

      Plinko rules

      Drop a ball from the top: at every row of pins it bounces left or right, and the slot it lands in multiplies your bet. The edges pay the most but are hit the least. Higher risk makes the edges bigger and the middle smaller; more rows give the ball more room to wander. You can drop several balls at once. Space drops a ball.

      Pick a bet and help the chicken cross.

      Chicken rules

      Put down a bet and send the chicken across ten lanes of traffic, one hop at a time. Every lane it survives raises the multiplier; cash out whenever you like, or risk the next hop. Get hit and the bet is gone. Easy: about 1 in 12 hops ends badly, Medium about 1 in 6, Hard about 1 in 4, and the payouts grow to match. Keys: Space hops, C cashes out.

        All bets

          Horse racing rules

          Every three minutes six horses race, the same race for everyone. For two minutes you can bet on as many horses as you like (up to 12 bets per race). Win pays if your horse comes first; Place pays if it comes first or second, at lower odds. The odds are fixed when you bet: the favourite pays least, the outsiders most. When betting closes the gates open and everybody watches the race live; winnings land on your balance as soon as the race is over, even if you left the page.

          On board

            Rocket rules

            One rocket for everyone. For 25 seconds you can board it with a bet (and, if you like, an automatic cash-out). Then it lifts off and the multiplier climbs faster and faster: about 2× after 5 seconds, 10× after 17 seconds. Cash out whenever you like and your bet pays that multiplier; if the rocket explodes first, the bet is gone. The higher it goes, the likelier the explosion: about 42% of rockets make it past 2×, one in twelve past 10×, and about one in seven blows up right on the pad. But one rocket in 300 is golden: you see it at liftoff, and it flies past 30× for sure. The few that reach 200× land on the moon and pay 200× to everyone still on board. Space boards and cashes out.

            Welcome to Lucky Burger!

              How the job works

              Lucky Burger costs nothing. Start a shift and do the work that comes up: scrub stains off the floor, take the fries out when the bar is in the green, flip and serve the patties at the right moment, put exactly what a customer ordered on the tray, and later as a boss give the crew the right answer, reorder stock when the shelf is low (yellow) and approve only reports whose numbers match. Every position has its own jobs. Every 10 seconds you get your wage, but only if you did something in those 10 seconds and the boss is not angry. The happier the boss, the bigger the paycheck: from 80% up to 120% of the wage. Finished jobs make the boss happier; things you let slip (a stain someone slips on, burnt food, a customer who walks out) make the boss angry. Paid time counts as time worked, and time worked brings promotions: from cleaner (14 credits every 10 seconds) up to store manager (250 credits every 10 seconds) after 2 hours 15 minutes of work. Higher positions pay more and bring new kinds of work. A shift ends when you press End shift, leave the game, switch to another tab or do nothing for 45 seconds.

              Pick a side, set your bet and flip.

              Coin flip rules

              Pick heads (crown) or tails (star), set a bet and flip. If the coin shows your side the stake doubles, and you choose: cash out, or let it ride and flip again for ×4, ×8, ×16 and so on. One wrong side and everything on the coin is gone. You may switch sides between flips. Ten wins in a row (×1,024) is the most a coin can carry: then it pays out by itself. Once in 10,000 flips (0.01%) the coin lands on its edge: then the stake on the coin is multiplied by 10, whichever side you picked. Space flips the coin.

                How the exchange works

                Six shares are traded, at the same prices for everyone. Every 5 minutes each share takes one step: up with its own chance (shown next to the chart), otherwise down. The safe shares rise often but only a little; the wild ones rarely, but then a lot. Now and then breaking news moves a share by much more. Buying costs the price plus a 1% fee, selling brings the price minus 1%. One order can cost at most your bet limit, and you can put at most 25 times your bet limit into one share. If a share falls below 5% of its starting price, the company goes bankrupt and its shares are lost; if it reaches 10 times its starting price, it is taken over and every holder is paid out at that price, even while offline. Then the share starts over at its starting price. Your shares stay in your portfolio while you play other games or log out.

                No-limit Hold'em · blinds 10/20

                Poker rules

                No-limit Texas Hold'em, blinds 10/20. Everyone gets two cards; five shared cards come in three rounds (flop, turn, river). Make the best five-card hand from your two cards and the board, or make everyone else fold. Each betting round you can fold, check or call, or raise (at least the last raise). Hands, best first: royal flush, straight flush, four of a kind, full house, flush, straight, three of a kind, two pair, pair, high card. Your buy-in comes from your credits; leaving the table puts your chips back. At the solo table the house takes a rake of 5% from every pot you win (at most 400 chips). Online: up to 8 players share one table and one dealer, and you have 25 seconds per move; missing two moves in a row sits you out. Keys: F fold, C check/call, R raise.

                Blackjack rules

                Get closer to 21 than the dealer without going over. Number cards count their value, J, Q and K count 10, an ace counts 1 or 11. A blackjack (ace plus a ten-card as your first two cards) pays 3 to 2; other wins pay 1 to 1, a tie returns your bet. The dealer draws to 16 and stands on every 17. Double: double your bet for exactly one more card. Split: two cards of the same value become two hands with their own bet (once per round; split aces get one card each). Six decks, shuffled when a quarter is left. Keys: H hit, S stand, D double, P split, Space deal.
